# RESURRECTION — Narrative Bible V1.0
> Status: approved creative baseline; not a claim that P4 already implements this story.
> Title: Resurrection — The Summer We Never Finished / 那个没有完成的夏天
> Genre: stylized 3D narrative exploration, digital archaeology, environmental puzzles.
> Emotional direction: complex and realistic, with playful discovery before bittersweet understanding.

## Creative thesis
A daughter reconstructs the digital world her late mother made as a teenager. She discovers not a perfect mother, but a funny, ambitious, flawed young person with friendships, mistakes and unfinished dreams. Recovering records is not resurrecting a person. Preserving evidence is not permission to invent the past.

**Player promise:** Each discovery changes what Mara understands; each repair visibly changes the world; no convenient perfect reconciliation.

## Logline
After Elena dies, 26-year-old Mara finds her old computer and the USENET handle `skylark`. Surviving posts and attachments reconstruct a half-finished 1998 coastal game, *Last Light*, co-created by Elena and June. As Mara repairs its lighthouse and discovers June's credit erased from a later build, she must separate technical loss from deliberate deletion and decide how to preserve an incomplete history.

## Ontology and limits
- **Real world:** Elena's lived-in study and Mara's grief; domestic objects matter as much as archives.
- **Archive world:** a modern interactive visualization of message IDs, reply chains, surviving attachments, divergent server copies, and provenance.
- **Game world:** a runnable reconstruction of the teenagers' fictional game *Last Light*.
- Theo is a scripted fictional NPC, **not** a conscious dead person. No AI resurrection of Elena; no supernatural claims.
- Historical facts, reasonable inferences, unknowns, and newly authored commemorative content must remain distinguishable.

## Character bible
### Mara, 26 — playable protagonist
Wants definite answers about her mother; needs to accept a parent as an autonomous, imperfect person. Clever, investigative, impatient with ambiguity. Arc: from “why did she do this?” to “I cannot know everything, but I can honor what is evidenced.”

### Elena / skylark, 17 in 1998
Programmer, stubborn, funny, creative, defensive when ashamed. Wants to make a game of her own; fears that leaving means losing that identity. She left the project under school/family financial pressure, later deliberately removed some of June's work/credit from a public build. Do not absolve all of her actions with a secret sacrifice.

### June / junebug, 17 in 1998
Artist, character writer, co-author. Wants public recognition of their joint creation and ownership of her contribution. Expressive, ambitious, sometimes overidentifies friendship with collaboration. Has a life independent of Elena. Do not reduce her to a twist, villain, or Elena's conscience.

### Theo — *Last Light* NPC
Lighthouse keeper created by June's writing and Elena's code. Earnest, comically dutiful, with unfinished behavior logic. His “waiting” is a programmed state, not a trapped soul. He must be funny and lovable before the lighthouse payoff.

## Canonical chronology (subject to evidence-consistent refinement)
| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| Spring 1998 | Elena and June meet through a hobbyist newsgroup and begin *Last Light*. |
| Summer 1998 | Town mostly built; lighthouse incomplete; two authors' credits intact. |
| Autumn 1998 | Elena leaves under school/economic pressures; a painful argument follows. |
| 1999 | June continues art/story; communication fades. |
| 2004 | Elena revisits a public build and removes some June attribution and assets. |
| 2005 | June challenges the removal; Elena does not adequately respond. |
| Years later | Elena drafts but never sends a note to June; preserves some originals. |
| Present | Elena is dead; Mara investigates the surviving material. |

Avoid presenting a current message from junebug as proof of supernatural return. If June appears in the present, she is alive and autonomous.

## Five-act structure
1. **A mother I didn't know** — study; discover skylark; surprise and humor.
2. **The unfinished summer** — reconstruct *Last Light*; meet Theo; love the world before judging its creators.
3. **Light the lighthouse** — compare distributed copies; rebuild parts and logic; physically align light; a triumph exposes suspicious deleted credit.
4. **The missing co-author** — distinguish corruption from deliberate change; reconstruct attribution and conflict from verifiable records; Elena did wrong, June's account matters.
5. **Unsent** — find a draft apology that June never received; archive verified history, explicitly mark uncertainty; optionally create a separate, labeled memorial edition. Return to the childhood drawing of a crooked lighthouse. No magical reconciliation.

## Emotional structure
Curiosity → delight → accomplishment → doubt → grief/complexity → quiet warmth. Avoid grief-heavy opening and manipulative monologues.

## Key dialogue drafts (English source, Chinese localization required)
**Theo first meeting:** “The lighthouse is almost ready.” / “How long has it been almost ready?” / “That's an excellent question. Please don't ask it again.”
**Deletion reveal:** “I thought the archive was broken.” / “The deletion was intentional.” / “By whom?” / “skylark.” Only reveal after the player has inspected supporting records.
**Ending:** “Did they finish it?” / “No.” / “Oh.” / “But look what they made.”

## Ending rules
The lighthouse may be repaired; the past cannot. June's credit is restored where evidence supports it. An unsent letter remains unsent. Player can (A) archive verified records with unknowns labeled or (B) separately make a clearly labeled commemorative creative edition. Neither is a moralized good/bad ending. Never fabricate a completed reconciliation.

## Narrative prohibitions
No dead-person chatbot, ghost horror, convenient perfect-mother absolution, melodramatic death montage, secret letter that explains everything, moral score, or ambiguous reconstructed data passed off as fact.

## Open writing work before production

- Give Theo an independent comic micro-arc.
