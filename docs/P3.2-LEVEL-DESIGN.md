# P3.2 level design: Chapter 1, "The Summer She Was Seventeen"

Game: **Resurrection — The Internet Remembers You**. The player is Mara, searching an old web game her late mother Elena made in 1998 (SUMMER98.EXE). Every number below is read from `frontend/p3/data.js`, `puzzle.js` and `engine.js`, and the rules are checked by `test/p3-core.test.js`.

The goal is not to bring Elena back. It is to rebuild a life from what survives, and to find out who she was before she was "Mom".

## Flow (target 5–8 minutes of play)

| Zone | Scene | What the player does | Rough time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Old study | Walk around, press E near objects: a book falls, a cassette is taken, a photo, a TODO list, a lamp. Use the glowing CRT. | 0:00–1:00 |
| B | Summer meadow | Move and jump, collect stars, pet the cat, read Elena's signs, cross a stream and a gap. Optional high route to a secret garden. | 1:00–2:30 |
| C | Broken-bridge canyon | Find the missing bridge and the "B3" blueprint. Notice the server door on the ledge under the cliff, found by dropping down toward the star. | 2:30–3:00 |
| D | Server room | Open three server copies, compare them, choose up to four fragments. Optional floppy disks on high shelves. | 3:00–5:00 |
| C | Bridge | Place fragments in the gaps, with retries. Planks drop in one by one. Cross. | 5:00–6:00 |
| E | Seaside | New ground, a crab, the end sign, the pier terminal. Elena's message and Mara's reply. | 6:00–7:00 |
| — | Memory island | The island grows in five stages. Walk around it and touch the memories. | 7:00–8:00 |

The times are a design estimate. **They have not been measured with a human.** The automated run times are in `docs/P3.2-TEST-REPORT.md` and are not a substitute.

The first interaction is available in the first seconds (the shelf is next to the spawn point). The core gameplay (movement, jump, stars, signs) starts at about 0:45 when the CRT is used. The test asserts first interaction < 30 s and core gameplay < 2 min.

## Map configuration

Units are tiles of 16 pixels. The picture is 320 × 176 px (20 × 11 tiles) and is scaled with crisp pixels.

| Scene | Size (tiles) | Spawn (x, y) | Scrolls |
|---|---|---|---|
| study | 20 × 11 | 2.5, 9 | no |
| world (zones B, C, E) | 122 × 14 | 3.5, 10 | horizontally and vertically |
| server | 40 × 11 | 3.5, 9 | horizontally |
| island | 32 × 11 | 8.5, 8 | horizontally |

Interaction range: **1.6 tiles** along x (and less than 1.2 tiles of height difference, so objects on another ledge are ignored). Pickup radius 0.9 tiles.

Solid tiles: `# G E R r S s D L P x F T` (ground, rock, sand, deck, ledge, bridge plank, server floor and tray). Everything else is open air. The map sides are solid, the sky above is open, and falling below the last row sends the player back to the last checkpoint.

A fall costs a few seconds and a counter tick. There are no lives and no game over.

### Movement

Fixed 60 Hz steps, no randomness. Run 7 tiles/s, jump apex 3.5 tiles, 7 frames of "coyote" time after leaving an edge, 7 frames of jump buffering before landing. The tests measure the jump windows: the stream and the second gap each allow about 3 tiles of take-off position, and every hop on the high route allows at least 1.2 tiles.

## The memory restoration puzzle

The world is "build 3". It has a gap with three slots, left to right: **1** (3 wide, flat), **2** (4 wide, flat), **3** (3 wide, last tile one row higher).

Three server copies remember the bridge differently:

| Copy | Build | Pieces | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mirror | 2 (Mar 1998) | left, middle, right | A **complete** bridge, in old sepia with sagging boards and "B2" stamps. The tempting trap. |
| Archive | 3 (Aug 1998) | left, middle, right | Left and right are clean. The middle is **damaged** (glitch). |
| Lab | 3 (Aug 1998) | middle, arched middle | A clean flat middle, and an **arched** one that does not fit. |

The solution is `a-left` → gap 1, `l-mid` → gap 2, `a-right` → gap 3. It needs two different copies. The player carries at most **4** fragments, and there are 8, so taking everything is impossible and the copies must be compared.

A placement is checked in a fixed order, and each failure has its own icon and short text:

1. **Build** — the stamp on the piece must equal the world's build (3). Fails for the whole Mirror copy.
2. **Damaged** — the data must be intact. Fails for the Archive's middle.
3. **Length** — the piece must be as wide as the gap.
4. **Edges** — the profile must match. Fails for the arched piece, and for a left piece put in the raised right gap.

Observation and reasoning: the player reads the build stamp on the blueprint sign and in the restore view, compares the three copies and their stamps and damage, decides which pieces are worth the four slots, and checks the raised right-hand end against the blueprint.

Recovery, so nothing can dead-end: a refused piece stays in the backpack and the player can try again immediately. A placed piece can be taken back by pressing its gap. The server room can be re-entered any time and pieces there are copies, never consumed, so a player who picked wrong pieces just goes back, puts them down and takes others. The browser test does exactly this.

Interaction: drag a fragment onto a gap (pointer events, so it works with mouse and finger), or tap/press a fragment and then a gap (keyboard, touch and switch users).

When the last piece fits, ten planks drop onto the canyon one at a time with a rising sound and sparkles, each becoming solid when it lands. The player cannot cross before the bridge is complete, and a bridge from wrong pieces is impossible by construction (a tampered save cannot skip the puzzle either; this is tested).

## Hidden and optional content

- **Secret garden**: four wooden ledges climb above the meadow to a platform with flowers, three stars and Elena's "cut content" note. It unlocks a sound, particles and a note object on the island. The first ledge is deliberately two tiles right of the stream so a player has a run-up.
- **Server ledge**: the door in the cliff, found by dropping toward a star.
- **Cat** (pet it), **crab**, **lamp**, **book that falls**, **three floppy disks** in the server room: all optional, all with feedback.
- **Cassette** (study) and **cat** and **garden note** appear on the island only if found, so the island reflects what the player noticed.

## Island

Five stages, in order: shore and ground, the cabin, trees and flowers, the old console that still switches on, then the things she kept (photo, cassette player, the star jar, the cat, the note). Star lanterns hang in the sky, one per star collected (up to 10). The island is a real scene: Mara walks it and touches each memory. The stage reached and every memory visited are saved.

## Accessibility and settings

Keyboard (arrows or A/D, Space, E), touch pad (buttons are at least 60 px tall), mouse and finger drag. Sound toggle and volume slider. Reduced motion (system setting or the button): no typewriter, no screen shake, no drifting clouds or sway, no dust, shorter growth and bridge timing. Text is DOM text (selectable, translatable), not drawn into the picture. The picture has a text label. The caption bar under the picture repeats everything said in the world.

## Difficulty review

- The first jump (stream) is 3 tiles wide with a 3-tile take-off window and a checkpoint 3 tiles before it.
- The only required precision jump after that is the one-tile step up at the end of the bridge, with a full tile of take-off tolerance.
- No timers, no enemies, no lives.
- Required text is short. No screen needs more than a sentence to continue. The longest text is Elena's first ending line, and it is the payoff.
- Dead ends removed: the server room and bridge are always reachable; falling returns to a nearby checkpoint; a full backpack explains itself; a wrong piece explains itself.
